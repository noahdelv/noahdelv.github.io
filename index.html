About Me

I'm a Computer Science student at the University of Michigan College of Engineering, graduating in August 2026. My work spans the full stack — from distributed systems and concurrent servers in C++ to React frontends and REST APIs — but I'm especially drawn to projects where engineering decisions directly shape how something feels to use.

I got into computer science through games, and game development remains a central part of how I think about software. Building a co-op underwater exploration game from scratch taught me more about systems design than most coursework: how input latency shapes feel, how UI communicates state under pressure, how a single mechanic can define or sink an experience. That thinking carries into backend work — I care about the clarity of an API, the responsiveness of a data pipeline, and the usability of whatever I'm building.

Currently, I work as an AI Data Specialist at Handshake, designing adversarial prompts to stress-test multimodal LLMs and building computer vision training datasets. I'm looking for roles in software engineering or game development where I can keep growing across systems, product, and design.

Outside of coding, I spend time playing and talking about games endlessly, making and listening to music, and staying active.
